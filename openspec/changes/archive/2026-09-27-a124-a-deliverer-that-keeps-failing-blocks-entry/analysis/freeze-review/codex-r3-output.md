D7’s normal successful-settlement paths have **no demonstrated lock-order deadlock**: settlement returns before `Gate.Block`, and mode projection occurs after commit (`alert_claim.go:331–334`; `operating_mode.go:468–476`). However, the blanket claim that journal methods accept no callbacks is false: `TransitionOperatingMode` invokes `Auditor.RecordAction` inside its transaction (`operating_mode.go:460–465`). The proof should identify actual callback bindings; the new executor escalation passes no auditor.

The acknowledgement interleavings are also not exhausted by “acknowledgement first” and “executor first.” Successful same-ID re-arm is correctly handled by token mismatch, but error outcomes erase that evidence, producing R4. Delayed escalation after human mode relaxation is explicitly possible in D:203–205; it remains conservative, but is not equivalent to escalation before relaxation and needs a consistent contract.

**D1, D2, D3 and timing**

- **D1:** The additive `Attempts` read is sound when performed through the same transaction and returned only after successful commit. Failed-attempt `NotFound` parity is correctly repaired. Add explicit SELECT-failure rollback and commit-failure tests; the shared helper also affects delivered settlement and lease release.
- **D2 — limit 3:** Adequately justified by `DefaultCriticalAttempts` and its rationale (`notifier.go:39–45`). The 4 s / 34 s comparison is valid for one uncontended row with zero ledger overhead, not general latency equivalence.
- **D3 — count missing publisher:** Adequately justified by existing fail-closed behavior (`exitwiring.go:62–70`) and a092’s requirement at its spec:76. The approximately 4–6 s delay is a single-row example, not a backlog bound.
- **D6:** With ten rows, 10 s failures and zero overhead, `C = 102 s`; first-row third failure is **214 s**, last-row third failure **304 s**, and adding maximum initial phase **316 s**. These are conditional calculations, not measurements or operational guarantees.
